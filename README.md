# Open Problems in Mathematics — a tier list

![Tier list](tier-list.svg)

**Ranking axis:** structural centrality × how much a proof would transform mathematics — *not* raw fame, *not* difficulty alone. Fame is a tiebreaker. This is why some household names land surprisingly low.

**What the axis measures — and what it doesn't** *(made explicit August 2026)*: tiers rank what a *full resolution* would buy. But mathematics mostly advances by tightening bounds, not closing conjectures — Zhang's 70,000,000 became 246 without twin primes falling, and that was the event of the decade in the field. Bound-movement is tracked inline in the entries and taken seriously in the [postmortem](#known-blind-spots-august-2026-postmortem) below; a problem's tier is not a claim that partial progress on it is worthless, and "no resolution" is not "nothing happened."

Inspired by the July 2026 counterexample to the **Jacobian conjecture** (Levent Alpöge + Claude, a 216-character polynomial map ℂ³→ℂ³ with constant Jacobian determinant that is not injective — n ≥ 3 refuted, n = 2 still open; verified across the community within a day of posting). One more reminder that "open" is a temporary condition — see the [graveyard](#-recently-fell-calibration-for-the-whole-list) at the bottom for what "solvable" has looked like lately.

> **Update — August 2026.** Eleven days after this list went up, OpenAI dropped [ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) from an internal model ("Astra"), each with a [Lean 4 certificate](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs) (zero sorries; independent checking via Comparator; journal review still pending). Headline: the **first non-sofic group** — open since Gromov posed soficity in 1999. Also fell: **Connes's rigidity conjecture** (refuted), **Ehrhart's volume conjecture** (proved), and **Erdős problems 183, 146, 180**. Bounds moved on four more fronts: sphere-packing density (upper bounds now reach the Cohn–Elkies threshold), binary/spherical codes (first exponential improvement in the upper bounds since MRRW 1977), the permanent (an n⁴/log n formula lower bound), and GapCVP hardness — plus exponential quantum parallel repetition. Details inline at entries [#59](#combinatorics--graphs), [#65](#combinatorics--graphs), [#78](#theoretical-computer-science) and in the [graveyard](#-recently-fell-calibration-for-the-whole-list). Scorecard for this list: nothing in S or A tier fell; four of the ten are counterexamples, so the [refutation-rate warning](#-recently-fell-calibration-for-the-whole-list) below stands. The batch also graded this list's *coverage*, and the grade was mixed — nine problems that belonged here all along are now in the [addenda](#b-addenda--added-august-2026), and the [postmortem](#known-blind-spots-august-2026-postmortem) explains how they slipped through.

On release day nobody outside has confirmed anything. **Every October note below is a claim**, tagged **Lean** or **paper-only**. The rankings stay frozen: nothing moves tier, and nothing enters the graveyard until others confirm it.

## S — Load-bearing pillars of mathematics

1. **Riemann Hypothesis (+ GRH for L-functions)** — thousands of theorems are conditional on it; secretly a statement about the "spectrum" of the primes. The one problem on both Hilbert's 1900 list and the Millennium list. *Oct 2026, claimed (**Lean**):* **the quasi-Riemann hypothesis.** ζ(s) and every Dirichlet L-function have no zeros with Re s > 7/8 (openai/math [003](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf)). Before this, no zero-free strip of *fixed* width was known at all. Corollaries: no Landau–Siegel zeros (separately Lean-stated) and a power-saving prime number theorem error term O(x^(7/8+ε)). RH itself is untouched and 7/8 is a long way from 1/2. But zero-free regions had barely moved since the prime number theorem: de la Vallée Poussin (1899) to Vinogradov–Korobov (1958), all shrinking toward Re s = 1. If this holds, it's the biggest step toward RH in well over a century. At the current pace that title may not last the year. The Lean statement is one line (`riemannZeta s ≠ 0` for `7/8 < s.re`), so statement fidelity isn't the worry; whether the proof checks is. OpenAI notes this work was an exception to their fixed pipeline, and that one write-up was human-edited.

   *Why "is it true?" is a clean binary here, unlike CH:* RH is equivalent to a Π⁰₁ arithmetic sentence — a "this computation never finds a counterexample" statement ([MathOverflow: Is RH equivalent to a Π₁ sentence?](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/31846)). Concretely: RH ⇔ an elementary inequality about σ(n) and harmonic numbers ([Lagarias 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0008177)), and ⇔ a specific 744-state Turing machine never halting (Matiyasevich–O'Rear–Aaronson, in [Aaronson's Busy Beaver survey, §3](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/bb.pdf); see also [the blog post](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=2725)). For such sentences falsity is always provable — run the machine to the counterexample — so even a ZFC-independence proof would settle the market: independent ⇒ no counterexample ⇒ *true*. Contrast the [CH market](#logic--foundations) below, where "true" is genuinely a philosophy question.

## Known blind spots (August 2026 postmortem)

The Astra batch was an unplanned audit of this list, and the misses weren't random. Four systematic biases, pre-registered now so we can grade them too:

- **Problem-shaped beat program-shaped.** Fields that frame their frontier as named conjectures (number theory, combinatorics) got dense coverage; fields that frame it as programs (birational geometry's MMP, geometric measure theory's singularity structure) got nearly none. We granted the program exception exactly once — Langlands, the most famous program in mathematics — and never again. Entries #106–108 are the partial correction.
- **Instrument bias.** The scaffolding this list leaned on — Wikipedia articles, Manifold markets, the formal-conjectures repo — is densest for crisp, discrete, certificate-shaped problems. Operator algebras and complex dynamics are thin in all three instruments, so they fell out of the sampling frame. The scaffolding wasn't decoration; it was quietly doing selection. (Look at the sparse link rows in the [addenda](#b-addenda--added-august-2026) — the bias is visible.)
- **The small-community tax.** Fame was the tiebreaker, and fame scales with community size, not problem importance. Operator algebras, computability theory, and coding theory all have high importance-to-fame ratios, and all three were missing entirely. Astra's two headline kills came from the first of these — the flood came through the least-watched valley.
- **The bounds genre.** A list of named conjectures implicitly frames progress as *resolution*. But six of Astra's ten results moved bounds or reached thresholds without closing any named conjecture — and that is the normal texture of mathematical progress, not the degenerate case. Sometimes a solution arrives from nowhere (Viazovska owed little to prior packing bounds); at least as often the bound-tightening *is* the field's mainline — Zhang → Maynard–Tao on prime gaps, ALWZ on sunflowers, Gilmer on union-closed, CGMS on diagonal Ramsey, and now Astra on MRRW, Cohn–Elkies, and the permanent. This list ranks what resolution would buy; it cannot rank where the next movement lands, and the next movement is disproportionately quantitative. The patch: bound-movement is annotated inline (#59, #60, #65, #78, #91, #111) and those annotations should be read as first-class content, not footnotes. Forecasting corollary: if you want to predict where AI hits next, don't scan tiers — scan for entries whose remaining distance is *quantitative*.

What survived the audit is worth stating too: the tier *rankings* took no damage — nothing in S or A fell or was grazed, all ten landed at B-magnitude or below. The rankings were right; the sampler had holes. Mountains correctly ranked, valleys unwatched. *(Left as written in August. See the October grade below.)*

## October 2026: a provisional grade

*Provisional twice over: the claims are unverified, and this was written on release day.* The rankings are frozen. July's tiers were our best attempt, and we score ourselves against them rather than revising them. Coverage patches like August's addenda are fair game; re-ranking because something got solved is not.

- **"Nothing in S or A fell or was grazed."** *Wrong within eight weeks.* A-tier #17 (Hilbert's 16th) is claimed resolved outright; #4, #5, #6 and #16 take major claimed partials; and S-tier #1 has a claimed fixed zero-free half-plane, in Lean.
- **The tiers as a gradient.** *Held.* Entries claimed fully settled: S 0/3 · A 1/15 · B about 13/75 (with addenda). C and cursed were always meant extensionally, as examples of a kind of problem rather than complete lists, and read that way **C took heavy fire**: Catalan's constant, π's irrationality exponent, Seymour, circulant Hadamard, short Egyptian fractions, Jacobsthal. "Famous but narrow" is exactly what a sweep of 4,000 problems hits. **Cursed held at its core** (Collatz, magic squares of squares, perfect cuboid and Brocard are untouched) but had softer borders than we thought: the Gaussian moat fell, and it had cursed energy.
- **"Betting 'true' is not free money."** *Holds.* About 4 of the ~17 claimed settlements here are counterexamples (Hadwiger, Sidorenko, Kaplansky zero divisors, Kuznetsov). The three "is it true?" markets that got claimed answers went 2 of 3 the market's way.
- **"Most exposed: Connes embedding for groups."** *Untested.* Nothing in the release touches hyperlinearity. The neighbors moved instead: free group factors, Gottschalk, Kaplansky direct finiteness.
- **"Scan for entries whose remaining distance is quantitative."** *Wrong about where hits land, right about where value sits.* Most of the haul was qualitative conjectures closed outright (Falconer, Mahler, Crouzeix, Cannon, UGC, Erdős APs). But the most consequential single claim is a partial result: quasi-RH, whose corollary of no Siegel zeros would make a large body of ineffective theorems effective.
- **Instrument bias and the small-community tax.** *Confirmed.* Five of the nine August addenda took claimed hits within two months.

**The flaw we can own: the axis was blind to direction.** "What would a resolution buy" never said whether it meant a proof or a refutation. Those buy different things. A proof of Hadwiger would have reorganized structural graph theory; a counterexample tells you minors don't control coloring, which is real but smaller. The Kaplansky zero-divisor counterexample lives in characteristic 2, away from the characteristic-0 Atiyah conjecture it might have threatened. With a quarter to two-fifths of recent resolutions arriving as counterexamples, a tier should have meant something like "value if true, value if false."

**Coverage, round two.** The [holding-pen line](#-recently-fell-calibration-for-the-whole-list) lists ~20 off-list claims. Some were real misses we record as misses: L = BPL (we listed only P = BPP), Hilbert–Smith, Kadison similarity, and θ(p_c) = 0 in 3D percolation, which we folded into a bundle. We are not adding them. August's addenda were problems that *hadn't* fallen; adding the ones that just did would turn a forecast into a scrapbook.

**Provenance, for honesty's sake.** The July list was a hasty attempt at a blind postdiction: Evan asked for it right after the Jacobian counterexample, prompting carefully so as not to reveal that the real question was where the Jacobian conjecture would land. It didn't really work. The list knew about the counterexample and filed it in the graveyard, never in a tier. (August's #107 note does eventually call it "affine geometry's recreational corner," which reads as roughly C.) So July was opinions written down early, not a controlled experiment, and August and October are where the pre-registration actually starts.
